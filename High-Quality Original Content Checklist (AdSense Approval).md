# High-Quality Original Content Checklist (AdSense Approval)

A 40-point checklist for writing original, experience-based, human content for a web dev / AI tools / automation blog. Ordered from most important to least important.

**Note:** Google doesn't ban AI-assisted writing. It penalizes unhelpful, unoriginal, mass-produced content. AI detectors are unreliable, so don't chase a "0% AI" score. Aim for real experience and real value.

## Tier 1: Critical (fail these and you get rejected)

1. **Real first-hand experience in every post.** Include something only you could know: your test, your error, your result. This is the core of E-E-A-T and what separates you from generic content.
2. **Original data or proof.** Add your own screenshots, logs, numbers, workflow JSON, or benchmarks. Proof can't be copied, and it shows you did the work.
3. **Zero copied text.** Never paste from other sites, docs, or forums. Copied text is the fastest route to rejection.
4. **Plagiarism scan before publishing.** Run Copyscape or Quetext. Even accidental overlap can look like copying.
5. **Solve a real problem completely.** The reader should finish the post without needing to search again. Incomplete answers read as low-value.
6. **Accurate, tested information.** Test every step and code snippet yourself. Wrong or untested info hurts trust and users.
7. **No unedited AI output.** If you use AI for outlining or grammar, rewrite it with your own experience. Raw AI text is a top cause of "low-value content" rejections.
8. **Substantial length with depth.** Aim for 1,000+ words where the topic needs it. Never pad. Depth means detail, not word count.
9. **Policy-safe topics.** No adult content, hacking-for-harm, pirated software, or misleading claims. A policy violation can mean rejection or a ban.

## Tier 2: High importance (build trust)

10. **Real author identity.** Use a real name, photo, and bio with your actual background (FalconXoft projects, Protonixs). Google wants a real human behind the content.
11. **About page with your story.** Explain who you are and why you're qualified. It's a major trust signal.
12. **Contact page with real details.** Include a working email. It shows the site is legitimate.
13. **Privacy Policy and Disclaimer pages.** AdSense requires privacy disclosures, and it's a quick win.
14. **Search-intent match.** Check what the top results for your keyword look like and make yours more useful or more specific. Content that misses intent gets ignored.
15. **A unique angle in the title and intro.** State what's new in the first 100 words (e.g., "I tested 4 setups with 100 leads"). It tells readers and Google why this post exists.
16. **Honest failures and limits.** Say what didn't work and when the method isn't a good fit. Real experience includes problems, and AI rarely produces them.
17. **Cite sources for facts you didn't create.** Link to official docs or studies and paraphrase them. It shows research and keeps you clear of plagiarism.
18. **Anonymize client info.** Never expose names or private data. It protects you legally and keeps trust.
19. **Consistent publishing history.** Publish steadily before applying (roughly 20-30 solid posts is the common guideline, though it's not official). A tiny or abandoned site looks unfinished.

## Tier 3: Medium importance (quality and readability)

20. **Clear structure.** Use H2/H3 headings, short paragraphs, and a summary at the top. People scan, and clear structure lowers bounce.
21. **Human voice.** Write in first person, with contractions and the way you'd explain it to a colleague. Stiff, generic phrasing reads as machine-made.
22. **Specific details over vague claims.** Say "response time dropped from 14 minutes to 2," not "it improved a lot." Specifics signal real experience.
23. **Remove AI filler phrases.** Cut lines like "in today's fast-paced world" and "it's important to note." They add nothing and are a giveaway.
24. **Original visuals.** Use your own screenshots and diagrams instead of stock images. They add proof and reduce duplication.
25. **Working code and templates.** Offer copy-paste snippets or downloadable files. They add usefulness that competitors lack.
26. **Internal links.** Link related posts together. It improves navigation and shows topical depth.
27. **Proofreading.** Fix typos and broken sentences. Sloppy writing lowers perceived quality.
28. **Updated dates and revisions.** Refresh old posts when tools change. Outdated tutorials lose value fast.

## Tier 4: Supporting (technical and site-level)

29. **Fast, mobile-friendly design.** Over half of readers are on phones, and a broken layout looks unfinished.
30. **Clean navigation and menu.** Visitors should find key pages in two clicks. "Navigation issues" is a known rejection reason.
31. **HTTPS (SSL).** The AdSense code and Google both expect a secure site.
32. **No broken links or 404s.** They signal neglect.
33. **No ads or popups cluttering the site before approval.** Too many ads before approval looks spammy, so keep the site clean.
34. **Descriptive meta titles and descriptions.** They help search appearance but don't decide approval.
35. **Image alt text.** It helps accessibility and image search.

## Tier 5: Least important (nice to have)

36. **Table of contents.** Helpful for long posts, but not a ranking factor.
37. **Schema markup.** Minor help for search display only.
38. **Social share buttons.** They have no effect on approval.
39. **Perfect keyword density.** Don't chase it. Write naturally.
40. **AI-detector score.** Treat it as a rough signal at most. Detectors give false positives on human text, and Google doesn't use them.

## Pre-publish quick test (30 seconds)

Before you hit publish, ask:

- Could anyone else have written this without doing the work? If yes, add proof.
- Did I include one failure, one number, and one screenshot?
- Does it answer the question fully?

If all three are yes, publish.
